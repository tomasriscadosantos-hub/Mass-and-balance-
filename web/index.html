How the checks work
  • Flying Academy OM (part C 1.2 and 1.4), worked out at the maximum take-off and landing mass: take-off 50 ft distance ×1.5 < TORA, landing 50 ft distance ×1.5 < LDA.
  • Student solo (OM part A 5.2.4): TORA at least 600 m, landing 50 ft distance ×1.67 < LDA (×1.92 on a contaminated runway), crosswind including gusts within 50 % of the maximum demonstrated unless the instructor has raised it.
  • Student solo weather minima (OM part C 5, day), at every aerodrome on the route: local flight 5 km and ceiling 1500 ft AGL, cross-country 8 km and 2000 ft AGL. Visibility 9999 counts as 10 km; type CAVOK, or "none" for no ceiling.
  • Daylight: cross-country flights land 30 min before sunset at the destination (OM part A), local flights before sunset. Sunset is worked out for the aerodrome's position and date.
  • Contaminated runway: the POH has no data for it, so the distances are for a dry runway and will be longer in reality.
  • Class B (old sheet), at the actual mass: take-off ground roll < TORA and 50 ft distance < TODA, then ×1.15 < TODA and ×1.3 < ASDA with a clearway or stopway, or ×1.25 < TORA without. Landing ground roll < LDA and 50 ft distance < 70 % of LDA.
  • POH corrections: −10 % for each 9 kt of headwind, +10 % for each 2 kt of tailwind (up to 10 kt). Dry grass: +15 % (take-off) or +45 % (landing) of the ground roll. Gusts count for the crosswind limit only.
  • Obstacles after take-off, from the AIP (AD 2.10): those in the take-off path (90 m either side at the end of the runway, widening by 12.5 % of the distance, out to 6 km). You are at 50 ft at the end of the POH take-off distance (with the wind, not factored), then climb at the POH best rate of climb; ground speed = climb speed (true airspeed) less the headwind. Less than 50 ft clearance is noted; none is a limit.
  • The POH take-off distances are for the short-field technique (flaps 10°). A normal take-off needs more runway: in the 172, flaps 0°, rotate 55 KIAS, climb 70–80 KIAS (school SOP); in the 152, flaps 0°, rotate 50 KIAS, climb 65–75 KIAS (school SOP).
  • Departure briefing, in the order of the SOP's departure crew briefing, under every take-off: TORR is the largest distance the rules compare with TORA; obstacles are those in the take-off path above, naming the one cleared by the least; ailerons into the crosswind. Vy is the POH best rate of climb speed at the aerodrome, Vx the POH best angle of climb speed. Engine failure after take-off in the 152: the POH's 60 KIAS, where the SOP gives 65 flaps up / 60 flaps down (the SOP says the POH applies where they differ).
  • Landing briefing, in the order of the SOP's arrival briefing, under every landing: LDR is the largest distance the rules compare with the LDA (for the class B rules, the 50 ft distance divided by 0.7); the obstacle named is the highest one the AIP lists under the approach. Final approach speeds from the school's SOP (the same as POH section 4), go-around from the POH balked landing. The POH landing distances are for the short-field technique (61 KIAS in the 172, 54 in the 152, maximum braking): a normal approach needs more runway.
  • Pressure altitude = elevation + (1013 − QNH) × 27 ft. Density altitude = PA + 120 ft per °C above ISA.
  • Fuel (OM part C 2.1): taxi + trip + alternate + 45 min final reserve. Trip and alternate each include 10 min for approach and landing. With stops, every leg is checked at its take-off with the fuel carried from the last refuelling, and the app says the least to load where you refuel. AVGAS is sold at LPPM, LPEV and LPCS only (AIP AD 2.4): LPFR and LPBJ have JET A1 only, LPSO no fuel.
